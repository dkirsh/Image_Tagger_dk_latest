# Reference support: fluency trajectory, stress of disfluency, clutter vs complexity, and the aha refund

### Annotated bibliography + synthesis · 2026-07-30

*Answers four questions with primary sources: (1) is there a perceptual-fluency **trajectory**? (2) how
much **stress** do people feel from clutter / visual complexity? (3) are **clutter-stress and
complexity-stress dissociable**? (4) once the stimulus becomes fluent, is there an **aha** that is pleasant
and stress-removing? Each entry says what the paper actually shows. Evidence flagged **firm** (replicated /
physiological) / **framework** (theoretical model) / **gap** (what is *not* yet directly tested).*

## Q1 — Fluency is dynamic, and affect tracks the *change* in fluency

- **Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman (2004), *Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure*** — the foundational
  claim that the *ease* of processing is itself hedonically marked and drives liking; fluency can come from
  many sources (contrast, symmetry, priming, repetition). **Framework/firm.**
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1207/s15327957pspr0804_3
- **Winkielman & Cacioppo (2001), *Mind at Ease Puts a Smile on the Face*** — the physiological anchor:
  more fluent processing elicits a fast **positive affective response measured as zygomaticus (smiling)
  facial EMG**, with reduced corrugator (frowning). Fluency → positive affect, within seconds, on the
  face. **Firm (psychophysiology).** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11761320/
- **Graf & Landwehr (2015), *A Dual-Process Perspective on Fluency-Based Aesthetics* (PACE model)** — the
  trajectory made explicit: an **automatic** route where fluency yields immediate liking, and a
  **controlled** route where *reducing* disfluency (working a stimulus until it resolves) yields **aesthetic
  interest/reward**. Affect depends on the fluency *dynamics*, not a static level. **Framework, well-cited.**
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1088868315574978
- **Graf & Landwehr (2017), *Aesthetic Pleasure versus Aesthetic Interest: The Two Routes to Aesthetic
  Liking*** — names the two outputs: **pleasure** from immediate fluency vs **interest** from
  disfluency-*reduction*. Directly relevant to "initial disfluency that then resolves." **Framework.**
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00015/full
- **Neuroelectromagnetic correlates of perceptual closure (J. Neurosci.)** — the "gestalt" is a **datable
  event**: a closure negativity (~230–300 ms) + gamma-band binding when a degraded image organizes. Gives
  the trajectory a measurable inflection point. **Firm.** https://www.jneurosci.org/content/30/24/8342

## Q2 — Clutter / visual complexity do produce measurable stress

- **Saxbe & Repetti (2010), *No Place Like Home: Home Tours Correlate With Daily Patterns of Mood and
  Cortisol*** — the strongest single result for your question: people whose language described their homes
  as **cluttered / unfinished** showed **flatter, less healthy diurnal cortisol slopes** (a physiological
  stress signature) and worse mood trajectories across the day, vs those describing restful/ordered homes.
  Clutter → measured cortisol. **Firm (physiological, real homes).**
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167209352864
- **Home clutter and mental well-being (J. Environmental Psychology, 2025)** — recent replication/extension:
  clutter predicts lower well-being, **moderated** by factors and **mediated by perceived home beauty** —
  i.e., the negative effect is not raw and is gated by order/aesthetics. **Firm, recent.**
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272494425001550
- **Tuch et al. (2009), *Visual complexity of websites…*** — higher visual complexity raised **physiological
  arousal** and worsened experience, task performance, and memory. Complexity → autonomic load in a task
  context. **Firm.** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S107158190900055X
- **O'Hare & Hibbard; Wilkins — visual discomfort** — images that depart from natural **1/f statistics**
  (excess mid-high-frequency energy: stripes, dense clutter) are **physiologically costly and aversive**
  independent of meaning. This is the *enduring*, low-level branch. **Firm.**
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1477153515612526

## Q3 — Yes: clutter-stress and complexity are dissociable (arousal ⟂ valence; order is the switch)

The literature is consistent that **complexity per se raises arousal, but whether that arousal is
*negative* (stress) or *positive* (fascination) is set by order / coherence / fluency** — so "cluttered"
and "visually complex" come apart precisely along the order axis.

- **Berlyne (collative variables) & "Berlyne Revisited" (Frontiers 2016)** — complexity drives **arousal**
  and preference follows an **inverted-U**; hedonic tone is **multifaceted**, not a single sign. Complexity
  is arousal-raising but valence-neutral until other variables resolve it. **Framework/firm.**
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00536/full
- **Pieters, Wedel & Batra (2010), *The Stopping Power of Advertising*** — the cleanest behavioral
  dissociation: **feature complexity** (disordered busyness ≈ clutter) *hurts* attention-to-brand and
  attitude, while **design complexity** (ordered elaboration) *helps* attention and comprehension. Same
  "complexity," opposite sign, split by order. **Firm.** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkg.74.5.048
- **Kaplan & Kaplan / Stamps** — preference = **complexity gated by coherence**; complexity without
  coherence is aversive, complexity with coherence is engaging. The environmental-psychology form of the
  same dissociation. **Framework/firm (meta-analytic).**

**Reading:** clutter ≈ *complexity without order/fluency* → the negative-arousal (stress) branch; rich but
ordered/legible complexity → positive-arousal (fascination/interest) branch. The two are dissociable, and
the dissociating variable is exactly the "order/legibility/fluency" axis the review's vector isolates.

## Q4 — Yes: gestalt resolution ("aha") is pleasant and can remove the stress

- **Muth & Carbon (2013), *The Aesthetic Aha: On the pleasure of having insights into Gestalt*** — when a
  hidden/ambiguous image suddenly **organizes into a Gestalt**, viewers report **pleasure**; detecting the
  Gestalt (the "aha") is itself liked. Direct evidence that closure is rewarding. **Firm.**
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001691813001121
- **Muth, Ebert, Marković & Carbon (2019), *"Aha"ptics*** — the aesthetic-aha pleasure generalizes beyond
  vision (haptic exploration), i.e., it's about the *resolution event*, not the modality. **Firm.**
- **Graf & Landwehr (2015/2017)** — the mechanism: **disfluency-reduction** (working through initial
  difficulty to resolution) is a distinct source of aesthetic reward ("interest"), over and above static
  fluency. The refund is real and can overshoot the baseline. **Framework.**
- **Eureka-effect EEG dynamics (2023)** — the insight/"aha" moment has a distinct oscillatory signature,
  consistent with a sudden state change (reward/relief), not gradual. **Firm (EEG).**
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10321100/
- *(Related, from insight research: aha solutions carry positive emotion and are better remembered —
  Danek et al. — supporting the "pleasant + sticky" character of resolution.)*

## Synthesis — the answer to your question

Putting the four together yields exactly the trajectory you proposed, **with one crucial boundary
condition**: whether resolution refunds the stress depends on whether a Gestalt is *available* to resolve.

1. A disfluent, cluttered, or over-complex stimulus first raises **arousal**, and where it is disordered
   (no coherence, or departing from natural image statistics), that arousal is **negative — measurable as
   cortisol (Saxbe & Repetti), skin-conductance/arousal (Tuch), and corrugator/discomfort (Winkielman &
   Cacioppo; Wilkins).** That is the initial stress, and it is genuine, not just a rating.
2. Clutter-stress and complexity are **dissociable**: complexity raises arousal, but the *sign* is set by
   order/coherence/fluency (Berlyne-revisited; Pieters feature-vs-design; Kaplan complexity×coherence). So
   "how much stress" from a complex scene is really "how much stress from its *disorder*," not its richness.
3. If a Gestalt then **resolves** — the image organizes, the model closes — fluency jumps, the change is
   **hedonically marked as pleasure**, and the initial negative affect is refunded, sometimes into the
   positive **aesthetic aha** (Muth & Carbon; Graf & Landwehr; Eureka EEG). Initial stress, **no enduring
   stress.**
4. If there is **no Gestalt to close** — the discomfort is baked into low-level statistics (stripes, dense
   clutter, 1/f departures) — recognition changes nothing and the stress **endures** (Wilkins; O'Hare &
   Hibbard). Initial *and* enduring stress.

**The gap (and our study).** No study we found tracks the *full within-trial arc* — disfluency → resolution
→ relief — **physiologically, for scenes/clutter specifically** (the aha work uses art/ambiguous figures;
the cortisol/arousal work uses static exposure). That is precisely the experiment the review proposes: a
time-resolved EMG/SCR/pupil design contrasting **gestalt-resolvable** vs **statistically-baked-in**
disorder, with recognition latency marking the predicted refund. The literature strongly predicts the
outcome; nobody has run exactly this contrast. That is the publishable opening.
