# Mechanisms — how presence of others acts (the index)

*Effects belong to mechanisms, not to raw headcount. Each mechanism: what it drives, the
mechanism, its sign, the key moderators, the evidence grade, and sources. The MATRIX
points here; entries and the tagger's occupancy register resolve their claims here.*

## 1. Social facilitation / inhibition (mere presence) → task performance
- **Impact:** presence of others (audience or co-actor) **improves simple/well-learned
  task performance and impairs complex/novel task performance.** Sign flips on task
  complexity.
- **Mechanism:** presence raises arousal (Zajonc's drive account) and/or
  evaluation-apprehension and attentional conflict; arousal strengthens the *dominant*
  response — correct on easy tasks, wrong on hard ones.
- **Moderators:** task complexity (the master switch); whether one can be evaluated.
- **Evidence: firm.** Bond & Titus meta-analysis of 241 studies; one of social
  psychology's most replicated effects (mechanism still debated — see contested note).
- Refs: [Bond & Titus 1983, 241-study meta-analysis (PsycNet)](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1984-01336-001) · [Zajonc 1965, *Social Facilitation* (Science)]

## 2. Density → crowding stress → performance & withdrawal
- **Impact:** high **density** (people/area) can become **crowding** (the stress
  experience) → physiological stress, worse performance on demanding tasks, and social
  withdrawal / reduced helping.
- **Mechanism:** density ≠ crowding — Stokols' distinction. Density becomes crowding when
  it also removes **control**, **predictability**, and desired **privacy**; overload +
  loss of control drives the stress. Chronic (home/institutional) crowding differs from
  acute.
- **Moderators:** control, predictability, meaning of the setting, chronic vs acute,
  culture.
- **Evidence: firm** (density→physiological stress; crowding→withdrawal); the density-vs-
  crowding distinction is foundational.
- Refs: [Crowding and stress: review of variables and theories (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02684491) · [Epstein 1981, Crowding Stress and Human Behavior (J. Social Issues)](https://spssi.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-4560.1981.tb01060.x)

## 3. Social buffering → stress reduction (the positive sign)
- **Impact:** the **supportive** presence of trusted others **dampens the stress
  response** — lower cortisol and cardiovascular reactivity, faster recovery. Opposite
  sign to crowding.
- **Mechanism:** perceived support / safe attachment down-regulates the HPA axis and
  threat appraisal; effect depends on relationship quality, not headcount.
- **Moderators:** relationship (trusted vs stranger), individual social anxiety (high
  social anxiety weakens or reverses buffering), developmental history.
- **Evidence: firm** (developmental/experimental cortisol work), with the social-anxiety
  qualifier now well documented.
- Refs: [Gunnar 2017, Social Buffering of Stress — a career perspective (Perspectives Psych Sci)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691616680612) · [Hostinar & Gunnar, social buffering of the HPA axis (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26230646/) · [Social anxiety and the stress-buffering potential of social presence (PLOS ONE)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0325303)

## 4. Proxemics / personal space → comfort, distance-scaled stress
- **Impact:** discomfort and stress scale with **interpersonal distance**, not just count;
  personal-space intrusion by strangers is arousing/aversive.
- **Mechanism:** Hall's proxemic zones (intimate/personal/social/public); violation of the
  expected zone for a relationship triggers arousal and avoidance.
- **Moderators:** **culture** (contact vs non-contact cultures), gender, relationship,
  setting norms.
- **Evidence: framework** (robust as a model; specific distances are culture-bound).
- Refs: Hall 1966, *The Hidden Dimension* (proxemics).

## 5. Territoriality & perceived control → whether presence is stressful
- **Impact:** control over one's space and over interaction (being able to regulate
  contact) determines whether others' presence reads as tolerable or stressful.
- **Mechanism:** Altman's privacy-regulation / territoriality — stress comes from a
  *mismatch* between desired and achieved privacy, in either direction (crowding *or*
  isolation).
- **Moderators:** ability to withdraw, markers of territory, role/status in the space.
- **Evidence: framework.**
- Refs: Altman 1975, *The Environment and Social Behavior* (privacy regulation).

## 6. Evaluation / audience & being observed → self-regulation, choking
- **Impact:** being **watched or evaluated** raises self-focus; can improve effort but
  cause "choking" on complex skilled tasks; independent of raw density.
- **Mechanism:** evaluation apprehension + explicit-monitoring; overlaps §1 but keyed to
  *observation*, not mere presence.
- **Evidence: firm (audience/choking effects); framework (mechanism partition).**

## 7. Emotional / behavioural contagion → mood, crowd behaviour
- **Impact:** affect and behaviour spread through co-present others — calm or agitation,
  helping or aggression — shaping the collective mood of a space.
- **Mechanism:** automatic mimicry + social appraisal; at scale, deindividuation/anonymity
  can lower behavioural restraint.
- **Evidence: framework** (contagion firm in lab; large-crowd behaviour more contested).

## 8. Composite: neurodiverse social load
- **Impact:** for many autistic/socially-sensitive people, co-presence, unpredictability of
  others, and forced proximity are **primary** stressors — often larger than the physical
  sensory load.
- **Mechanism:** bundles §2 (density/predictability), §4 (proximity), §6 (being observed)
  with reduced ability to buffer via §3.
- **Evidence: framework (applied, growing).** Cross-refs the materials §8 sensory-load
  index; here the load is social, not surface.
