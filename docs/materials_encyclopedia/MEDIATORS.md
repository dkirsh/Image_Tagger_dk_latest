# Mediating properties → impacts (the index)

*Impacts belong to physical properties, not to materials. This is the master index: each mediating property,
what it drives, the mechanism, the evidence grade, and the key sources. Material entries point here.*

## 1. Acoustic absorption (α) → reverberation, speech intelligibility
- **Impacts:** speech intelligibility; children's attention & memory; learning; listener *and* speaker
  cognitive load; open-plan office performance + stress. **Direction:** absorptive (soft/porous) → shorter
  reverberation → higher STI → better cognition; reflective (hard) → worse.
- **Mechanism:** reverberation smears speech; low-SNR/low-STI raises effortful listening → drains working
  memory.
- **Evidence: firm.** Mealings 2022 (attention/memory review); simulated-classroom speech recognition;
  reverberant cognitive-load study.
- Refs: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1351010X221104892 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3283898/ · https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-70820-w

## 2. Surface reflectance & gloss → glare, visual comfort
- **Impacts:** visual discomfort/glare; sensory overload (esp. neurodiverse); daylight distribution/mood.
  **Direction:** high gloss/specularity → glare → discomfort; matte, mid-reflectance → comfort.
- **Mechanism:** specular highlights + luminance extremes → discomfort glare; links to the 1/f-departure
  discomfort channel in the clutter work.
- **Evidence: firm (glare/comfort); framework (mood).**
- Refs: (visual-discomfort/glare literature; cross-ref clutter `spectral_discomfort`).

## 3. Fractal dimension of surface pattern → restoration, preference
- **Impacts:** stress reduction / autonomic restoration; aesthetic preference; attention restoration.
  **Direction:** mid-range fractal D (~1.3–1.5) → most preferred + calming; very low or very high → less so.
- **Mechanism:** "fractal fluency" — visual system tuned to natural fractal statistics; efficient processing.
- **Evidence: firm (preference/arousal); framework (clinical stress).** Individual/cultural differences real.
- Refs: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Reduction-of-Physiological-Stress-Using-Fractal-Art-Taylor/22a400600388b8ab6644cb9864db82769cb60c37 · https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4939-3995-4_30 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27252634/

## 4. Thermal effusivity (warm/cold to touch) → comfort, affect, embodied judgment
- **Impacts:** tactile comfort; warmth/coziness affect; (contested) "social warmth" priming.
  **Direction:** low effusivity (wood, cork, textile → feel warm) → comfort/coziness; high (stone, metal →
  feel cold).
- **Mechanism:** rate of heat draw from skin sets perceived warmth; embodied-metaphor accounts extend it to
  social judgment.
- **Evidence: firm (perceived warmth/comfort); contested (social-warmth priming — replication concerns).**
- Refs: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3005631/

## 5. Surface hardness & roughness → haptic comfort, embodied judgment
- **Impacts:** seating/contact comfort; (contested) rigidity/difficulty judgments (hard→"harder"
  negotiator; rough→"difficult").
- **Evidence: framework (comfort); contested (priming).**
- Refs: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3005631/

## 6. VOC / chemical emission → cognition, health
- **Impacts:** cognitive-function scores; symptoms/SBS; long-term health. **Direction:** high-emitting
  finishes/adhesives/composites → worse cognition & health; low-VOC → better.
- **Mechanism:** VOC + CO₂ + poor ventilation depress higher-order cognition (crisis response, strategy,
  information use).
- **Evidence: firm.** Harvard COGfx controlled exposure.
- Refs: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4892924/

## 7. "Natural material" gestalt (wood, plants, stone) → stress, affect
- **Impacts:** reduced physiological arousal; improved mood; restoration; healthcare outcomes.
  Bundles mediators 2–5 (matte, mid-fractal, warm, absorptive) + semantic "nature."
- **Evidence: framework→firm (stress/affect); contested (cognitive performance — mixed).**
- Refs: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1420326X20927437 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S027249442300107X

## 8. Sensory load (composite: reflectance + acoustics + texture) → neurodiverse impact
- **Impacts:** sensory overload / regulation for autistic & sensory-sensitive people; ability to focus/stay.
  **Direction:** matte, sound-absorbing, low-contrast, predictable-texture → lower load.
- **Evidence: framework (applied, growing).** ASPECTSS index; scoping reviews.
- Refs: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283099110_Architecture_for_autism_Built_environment_performance_in_accordance_to_the_autism_ASPECTSS_design_index · https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/6/3203

## 9. Symbolic / semantic meaning → preference, appropriateness, valuation
- **Impacts:** likeability; perceived quality/status; beliefs about *what a material is for*; cultural
  variation. **Direction:** context- and culture-dependent (marble=luxury; wood=warmth/nature;
  concrete=cold/brutal; plastic=cheap) — but these are learned associations, not fixed.
- **Evidence: framework (design research, qualitative).**
- Refs: https://www.ijdesign.org/index.php/IJDesign/article/view/1965/693 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0261306909007110

## 10. (thin) Degradation / disorder of materials → prosociality / aggression
- **Impacts:** antisocial behaviour where materials read as neglected/vandalised (broken-windows); indirect.
- **Evidence: contested / indirect.** Flag, don't assert.
