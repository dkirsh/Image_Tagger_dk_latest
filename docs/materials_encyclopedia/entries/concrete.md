# Concrete (cast / precast, exposed)

**Class:** mineral, cementitious. **Uses:** structure, floors, exposed walls/ceilings, brutalist façades.

## Physical property profile
- Acoustic absorption (§1): **very low → highly reflective**; long reverberation; a primary driver of poor
  room acoustics unless treated.
- Reflectance & gloss (§2): matte, mid–low reflectance; low glare (polished concrete raises gloss/glare).
- Fractal dimension (§3): low surface fractality (uniform) unless board-formed/aggregate-exposed.
- Thermal effusivity (§4): **high → feels cold** to the touch; high thermal mass.
- Hardness/roughness (§5): very hard; cool, unyielding tactile.
- VOC (§6): **very low** emission itself (inert); good for IAQ.
- Natural-material gestalt (§7): weak/absent; reads as "built/industrial."

## Semantic / cultural meaning
Modernity, honesty-of-structure, permanence, but also coldness, institution, and (post-brutalism) either
prestige or oppression depending on culture and cohort. Beliefs about proper use: prized in galleries/civic
architecture; resisted in homes/care settings as "unhomely."

## Caveats / thin spots
Concrete's biggest *measured* human cost is acoustic (reverberation), which is fixable with added absorption —
so many "concrete feels bad" claims are really untreated-reverberation claims. Aesthetic valence is strongly
cultural; don't universalise.
