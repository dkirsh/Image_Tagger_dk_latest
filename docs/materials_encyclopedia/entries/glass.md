# Glass (glazing, glossy hard surfaces)

**Class:** vitreous, hard, specular. **Uses:** windows, partitions, façades, tables, screens; representative
of the broader **glossy/reflective hard-surface** family (polished stone, high-gloss lacquer, mirror, metal).

## Physical property profile
- Acoustic absorption (§1): **very low → reflective**; large glazed/glossy rooms are reverberant.
- Reflectance & gloss (§2): **high gloss / specular** — the defining property; strong glare + mirror
  reflections + luminance extremes.
- Fractal dimension (§3): near-zero surface fractality (smooth, featureless).
- Thermal effusivity (§4): **high → feels cold**.
- Hardness/roughness (§5): very hard, smooth.
- VOC (§6): glass itself inert/low; coatings/sealants vary.
- Natural-material gestalt (§7): absent; "engineered/pristine."
- (Glazing also mediates **daylight + view to nature**, a large positive wellbeing channel that belongs to
  the *view*, not the glass surface — keep separate.)

## Semantic / cultural meaning
Transparency, cleanliness, modernity, openness, prestige; also coldness, fragility, surveillance/exposure
(lack of privacy). Beliefs about proper use: prized for daylight, views, and "open" cultures; resisted where
privacy, warmth, or acoustic calm are needed.

## Caveats / thin spots
Keep two channels apart: the **glass surface** (glare, cold, reverberant — mostly costs) vs **glazing as an
aperture** to daylight and nature (large wellbeing benefit — Ulrich). Conflating them is the commonest error
in "glass is good/bad" claims.
